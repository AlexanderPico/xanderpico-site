The wave and the leash

Who should count the failures when people hand more of what they do to AI, and how can anyone tell that the counting is independent of the thing being counted?

An essay on the long line of capacities people have handed to tools and institutions, why most of us will not refuse the next handoff to AI, and who should independently count its failures. Includes Who counts?, a quarterly ledger of how AI is being checked.

By Alex Pico · Published November 16, 2026 · Markdown · Feed for this thread

AI & societyAI safety

Resources

  • ESSAYThe wave and the leashPeople have always handed parts of their lives to things bigger and faster than any one of us. AI may be the first handoff to reach the whole loop of sensing, remembering, learning, deciding and acting. Most people will not refuse it, so the practical question is who counts its failures, independently of the thing counted.
  • TRACKERWho counts? A ledger of how AI is checkedFor each standard, law, company framework, research method, tribunal ruling and insurance product that checks AI: who writes the definitions, who pays the checker, when the authority ends, whose evidence is used and what the checker loses if wrong. Quotations and links only, no grades. Refreshed quarterly.
