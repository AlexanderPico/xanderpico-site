# Passwords in the Age of AI

**Guiding question:** Which everyday habits matter most for keeping accounts safe now that AI makes familiar scams cheaper to run?

A four-page, printable guide for friends and family on keeping accounts safe now that AI makes familiar scams easier: the five ways accounts get opened, password managers and passkeys, a security-key setup, recovery, and a worksheet for the accounts everything else depends on.

## Key points

- A password manager should generate and autofill a different password for every account, with at least 20 random characters where the website allows it. Source: [Passwords in the Age of AI: a friends and family guide](https://xanderpico.com/resources/cybersafe/index.md)
- Caller ID, tone and personal details do not prove who is calling, because AI can imitate writing or a familiar voice. Source: [Passwords in the Age of AI: a friends and family guide](https://xanderpico.com/resources/cybersafe/index.md)
- An old phone number, recovery email, support process or carrier account can bypass a strong sign-in, so recovery methods need to be kept current and tested. Source: [Passwords in the Age of AI: a friends and family guide](https://xanderpico.com/resources/cybersafe/index.md)
- Each security key, including the spare, should be tested after signing out, because a spare that was never registered will not restore access. Source: [Passwords in the Age of AI: a friends and family guide](https://xanderpico.com/resources/cybersafe/index.md)
- Changing a password may not end an existing signed-in session, so other sessions and unknown devices also need to be removed after a suspected compromise. Source: [Passwords in the Age of AI: a friends and family guide](https://xanderpico.com/resources/cybersafe/index.md)

- 2025-08: [NIST Special Publication 800-63B](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html). NIST's authentication guideline says verifiers shall not impose password composition rules or require periodic password changes unless there is evidence of compromise, and that applications at assurance level 2 must offer a phishing-resistant option.
- 2025-05-15: [Senior US Officials Impersonated in Malicious Messaging Campaign](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250515). The FBI described a text and AI-generated voice messaging campaign, running since April 2025, that impersonates senior US officials, and said not to assume a message claiming to be from one is authentic.
- 2024-12-03: [Criminals Use Generative Artificial Intelligence to Facilitate Financial Fraud](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203).
