FEATURED PROJECT · IN DEVELOPMENT

AI-native biology,
beyond the announcements.

A series in development on the people and companies rebuilding biological discovery around AI, informed by hands-on experience in protein structure, electrophysiology, and bioinformatics. What are they betting on? How do their approaches differ? What changes as the work meets reality?

Read the project outline

Begin with biotech: compare founders’ and researchers’ accounts across companies and over time. Use full interviews and primary research to distinguish stated ambitions, demonstrated results, and open questions.

Organize future pieces by company, scientific approach, and shared question. Expand into materials and energy only when the biotech format proves useful.

No issues are published here yet. The series is in development. It is research and commentary, not investment advice or a company recommendation.

BACKGROUND

At the bench.
At the keyboard.

My work connects experimental science with computation: making and measuring materials, studying the neural systems behind attention, understanding how protein structures enable function, and building tools to make biological data useful. Collaborative disease studies connect that work to questions in cancer, infection, neurodegeneration, and cardiovascular and kidney disease.

I bring that combination of hands-on experimentation and systems-level thinking to new questions in AI, biology, materials, and energy.

DISEASE RESEARCH

Different diseases.
Connected questions.

Collaborative research across molecular mechanisms, genetics, immune responses, and data-driven analysis.

My coauthored work spans disease areas that are often studied separately, bringing experimental and computational perspectives to shared questions about how biological systems go wrong.

  • Brain cancer: molecular classification and genetic variation in glioma.
  • Infectious disease: HIV persistence and the inflammatory mechanisms of COVID-19.
  • Cardiovascular and kidney disease: circulating proteins, biological pathways, and heart failure risk.
  • Neurodegeneration: behavior, neuroinflammation, and Alzheimer’s disease models.
