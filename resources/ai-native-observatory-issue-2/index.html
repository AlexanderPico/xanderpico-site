When a company moves its own date

Public companies repeat their plans every year in their annual reports, so you can lay one year's plan beside the next and see what moved. This issue does that for Relay Therapeutics, Schrödinger and Absci. The full ledger, with every quote linked to its filing, is on the observatory page: https://xanderpico.com/resources/ai-native-observatory/

Dates move both ways. Relay's cash runway sentence changed every year:

  • FY2022: “cash, cash equivalents, and investments of $998.9 million as of December 31, 2022 will enable us to fund our operating expenses and capital expenditure requirements into 2025”
  • FY2023: “cash, cash equivalents, and investments of $750.1 million as of December 31, 2023 will enable us to fund our operating expenses and capital expenditure requirements into the second half of 2026”
  • FY2024: “cash, cash equivalents, and investments of $781.3 million as of December 31, 2024 will enable us to fund our operating expenses and capital expenditure requirements into the second half of 2027”
  • FY2025: “cash, cash equivalents, and investments of $554.5 million as of December 31, 2025 will enable us to fund our operating expenses and capital expenditure requirements into 2029”

Each later date is further out, while the cash figure in the same sentence went from $998.9 million to $554.5 million. The filings do not explain how those fit together here, and I haven't looked into it. My reading: a "moved" status is not a verdict. A date that moves later can be good news, bad news or a change in how the company counts.

Dates narrow, and programs end. Schrödinger's FY2023 report said “We anticipate reporting initial data from the Phase 1 clinical trial of SGR-2921 in late 2024 or 2025.” The FY2024 report narrowed that: “from each of the Phase 1 clinical trial of SGR-2921 in patients with relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia or high-risk myelodysplastic syndrome and the Phase 1 clinical trial for SGR-3515 in patients with advanced solid tumors in the second half of 2025.” Then the FY2025 report says “In August 2025, we announced the discontinuation of the clinical development program for SGR-2921” Next to that, another Phase 1 program kept its plan: “In June 2025, we reported initial clinical data from our ongoing Phase 1 clinical trial of SGR-1505 in patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell malignancies.” My reading: one company, one report, two programs, two different fates. A single line like "on track" would flatten that.

A plan changes after the milestone. Absci's FY2023 report planned ABS-101 this way: “we plan to submit an IND in the first quarter of 2025 and potentially initiate a Phase 1 clinical trial in the first half of 2025” The FY2025 report says: “following a favorable early safety profile and notwithstanding positive interim Phase 1 results as announced in November 2025, we made the strategic decision to seek a partner for ABS-101 rather than advance it through later-stage development ourselves” My reading: the plan changed even though, in the company's own words, the results were favorable. The ledger records what the company said, not why.

Recursion, a follow-up. From the Q2 2026 call, two statements with dates on them: “which we believe provides us with an operating runway through early 2028”, and “we just received IND clearance for REC-7735, positioning it to enter the clinic later this year.” Both can be checked against later filings, so both are marked open in the ledger.

How to read this. Plans are compared with the company's later statements, not with trial results. All entries are unreviewed machine-selected pairs with quotes matched word for word to the filings. Research and commentary, not investment advice.
