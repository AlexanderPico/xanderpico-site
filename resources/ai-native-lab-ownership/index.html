Same shortage, different labs

Four AI-first drug discovery companies name the same thing as scarce: accuracy in designing molecules. They own very different labs, and they can find out they were wrong at very different speeds.

How to read this. Placement is a reading of what each company’s own people said in interviews, talks and earnings calls. It is not a measurement and has not yet been checked by a human reviewer. Two to three sources per company. Quotes come from machine-made transcripts that have not yet been checked against the audio. Research and commentary, not investment advice.

Hollow dot = low-confidence reading. Recursion is shown for reference: its executive names a different limit (picking the right target), so it is not in the group.

What stands out

  • The same sentence, different clocks. Genesis: “if your accuracy is not sufficient, it will therefore not be useful for the downstream things that you care about, which is both potency prediction, but also prospective design.” BigHat describes learning in days: “We wanted a situation where we could do data science and machine learning today, and make predictions tomorrow, and know the truth of those the next day after.” Absci points to a dated clinical readout: “We'll have a 13-week interim readout on that later this year with the 26 efficacy readout, first part of 27.”
  • Owning a lab is not the same as naming the same shortage. Recursion says it runs a very large lab, yet its executive says “building the best drug isn't generally the number one limitation. It's picking the right target.”
  • A different arrangement. Manas AI: “Right now our focus is mainly on making molecular generators. So we have partnerships that do these other aspects of drug development.”
