Resource · path tracker

Frontier Labs as National Laboratories

Nine possible paths for how the US government and frontier AI labs might relate, each with signposts and an estimated probability of happening by 2030. Re-scored over time; every snapshot is kept. The first measurement is 2026-10-22; the three earlier quarter-end points (March, June, September 2026) are simulated retrospective estimates, drawn as hollow dots, not contemporaneous measurements. Paths are ordered by current estimate.

Snapshot 2026-10-22 · horizon by end of 2030 (2030-12-31)

Each probability is a separate estimate that its path happens by the horizon. Paths can co-occur, so they do not sum to 100%. Estimates: draft estimates by the research team.

A. Ad hoc levers continue (the status quo)

85%range 75–93%+2 pts vs simulated 2026-09-30

Ad hoc levers continue (the status quo). Testing stays voluntary, controls are applied model by model, and disputes go to court.

Resolves YES if

Resolves YES if between 2026-10-23 and 2030-12-31 the federal government applies at least two new firm- or model-specific measures to named frontier developers: an export control on named models, a procurement designation, or a negotiated pre-release access agreement.

id A-adhoc-levers · horizon 2030-12-31

B. Steering through conditions on chips and contracts

80%range 65–90%±0 pts vs simulated 2026-09-30

Steering through conditions on chips and contracts. The state governs frontier compute it does not own by attaching conditions to export licenses and investment agreements.[183][185] It is path G applied abroad first, and it is already running: Microsoft–G42 operates under an "Intergovernmental Assurance Agreement," and the 2025 G42 and HUMAIN approvals carry "security and reporting requirements."[183][185]

Resolves YES if

Resolves YES if between 2026-10-23 and 2030-12-31 there are at least three new publicly announced US export approvals, licenses or government-backed agreements for foreign AI compute that state security, reporting, know-your-customer or monitoring conditions, or a published federal rule that imposes such conditions.

Signposts

  • Emerging↑ intergovernmental assurance agreements reused as a template for other AI campuses as of 2026-10-22
    • Microsoft — Microsoft and G42 partner (Apr 15, 2024; $1.5B; Intergovernmental Assurance Agreement) [183]
    • US Department of Commerce — Statement on UAE and Saudi chip exports (Nov 19, 2025) [185]
  • Emerging↑ know-your-customer or monitoring terms written into export licenses as of 2026-10-22
    • Federal Register: Commerce proposed IaaS KYC rule (Jan 29, 2024) [84]
    • US Department of Commerce — Statement on UAE and Saudi chip exports (Nov 19, 2025) [185]
  • Not yet↑ the same conditions applied to domestic data centers (would also move G up) as of 2026-10-22
    • White House: EO 14409, Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security (Jun 2, 2026) [94]
  • Emerging↓ evidence that conditions are evaded, e.g. extraction through clouds and proxies as of 2026-10-22
    • CISA/NSA advisory AA26-251A: China-based AI companies' industrial-scale distillation campaigns (Sep 8, 2026) [126]
  • Not yet↓ approvals granted without stated conditions as of 2026-10-22
Proposers and analogues
International analogues
  • United Arab Emirates: Microsoft–G42 investment under an Intergovernmental Assurance Agreement [183]
  • United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia: Commerce approvals for G42 and HUMAIN conditioned on security and reporting requirements [185]

id B-conditional-compute · horizon 2030-12-31

C. Frontier labs as users and partners of national facilities

60%range 45–75%±0 pts vs simulated 2026-09-30

Frontier labs as users and partners of national facilities. AI follows the beamline pattern: government-owned compute (Solstice, Lux, Doudna) allocated by merit review, with company work handled as proprietary use at full cost recovery, CRADAs or Strategic Partnership Projects.[134][140][158] This is the path most grounded in how Argonne and Berkeley Lab already work.

Resolves YES if

Resolves YES if between 2026-10-23 and 2030-12-31 a named frontier developer is publicly documented training, fine-tuning or running frontier-model research on DOE-owned compute under a user agreement, CRADA, Strategic Partnership Project or merit allocation.

Signposts

  • Not yet↑ an INCITE-style call that allocates frontier-scale AI compute as of 2026-10-22
  • Emerging↑ Lux-type public-private co-investments repeated as of 2026-10-22
    • DOE — New public-private partnership model, two supercomputers (Lux and Discovery at ORNL, Oct 27, 2025) [144]
  • Emerging↑ Genesis awards moving from credits to company-led frontier-model work as of 2026-10-22
    • DOE — First Genesis Mission projects selected (278 awards, Jul 22, 2026) [147]
  • Emerging↓ user-facility openness 'without regard to nationality' colliding with access restrictions on frontier models as of 2026-10-22
    • Anthropic: Statement on the directive to suspend Fable 5 access (Jun 12, 2026) [111]
    • DOE Office of Science — User Facilities Frequently Asked Questions (definition of a user facility) [134]
Proposers and analogues
Who proposes it
  • Chessen and Martell (Lawfare): Apollo-style whole-of-society program [198]
  • Public AI writers (Mozilla, Public AI Network, Brookings): Public option across the AI stack [202][203][205]
  • Google: Advanced partnerships with national labs; export rules that allow exports [221] (interest: Cloud and AI-services exporter)

D. A national-champion bargain

30%range 15–45%±0 pts vs simulated 2026-09-30

A national-champion bargain. A Bell-style arrangement: protection, procurement and possibly equity in exchange for a public return.[101][109]

Resolves YES if

Resolves YES if by 2030-12-31 the US government acquires equity, warrants, a golden share or a contractual revenue share in a frontier developer, or signs an agreement granting it preferred status explicitly in exchange for a public return.

Signposts

  • Not yet↑ a finalized government equity stake, and whether it carries governance rights as of 2026-10-22
    • CNBC: Trump administration, OpenAI discussing possible government stake (Jun 5, 2026) [109]
    • Semafor: Trump advisers weigh structure of potential AI stakes (Jun 17, 2026) [110]
  • Not yet↑ licensing or access concessions attached to procurement as of 2026-10-22
  • Emerging↓ public rejection of stakes by companies or Congress as of 2026-10-22
    • Semafor: Trump advisers weigh structure of potential AI stakes (Jun 17, 2026) [110]

E. A public lab spins out a company

25%range 12–40%+5 pts vs simulated 2026-09-30

A public lab spins out a company. A government lab builds a capability and hands it to a new firm, as ITRI did with TSMC in 1987.[196] ITRI had earlier licensed IC technology from RCA in 1976 and spun off UMC in 1980.[196][197] The flow runs opposite to the thesis, from public to private.

Resolves YES if

Resolves YES if by 2030-12-31 a model trained at a DOE national laboratory, or the core team that built it, moves to a new company or nonprofit under a license, CRADA or similar agreement that is publicly announced.

Signposts

  • Not yet↑ a DOE science model such as AuroraGPT, or its team, moving into a company or nonprofit as of 2026-10-22
    • ALCF — Argonne releases Aurora exascale supercomputer to researchers (Jan 28, 2025) [141]
  • Not yet↑ Genesis platform tools licensed out under CRADA terms as of 2026-10-22
    • DOE — First Genesis Mission projects selected (278 awards, Jul 22, 2026) [147]
    • DOE Technology Transfer Working Group — Partnering Guide (CRADA, SPP; Dec 2024) [158]
  • Not yet↑ an explicit DOE spin-out policy for lab-trained models as of 2026-10-22
  • Emerging↓ frontier developers supplying the models the labs use as of 2026-10-22
    • DOE — First Genesis Mission projects selected (278 awards, Jul 22, 2026) [147]
Proposers and analogues
International analogues
  • Taiwan: ITRI transferred fabs, equipment, technology and staff to TSMC (1987); earlier UMC spin-off [196][197]

F. An AI FFRDC under an M&O contract

20%range 8–35%±0 pts vs simulated 2026-09-30

An AI FFRDC under an M&O contract. The government owns a secure AI facility and contracts an operator, possibly an LLC of companies and universities on the Triad or UT-Battelle pattern, with performance ratings and recompetes as the accountability tool.[148][149][152]

Resolves YES if

Resolves YES if by 2030-12-31 Congress or a federal agency establishes a new FFRDC, or awards a management-and-operating contract, for a government-owned secure facility whose stated mission includes frontier-AI development or evaluation.

Signposts

  • Not yet↑ legislation or appropriations naming an AI FFRDC as of 2026-10-22
  • Not yet↑ a DOE or DoD solicitation for an AI facility operator as of 2026-10-22
  • Not yet↑ the 2025 Action Plan's high-security data centers becoming a government-owned site as of 2026-10-22

G. The regulated circuit

15%range 7–30%±0 pts vs simulated 2026-09-30

The regulated circuit. Compute and model access are licensed, covering both training runs and distillation through APIs.[125][126]

Resolves YES if

Resolves YES if by 2030-12-31 a federal statute or final rule requires licensing, registration with approval, or mandatory pre-release access for frontier models or large AI data centers, or imposes know-your-customer duties on compute or API customers.

Signposts

  • Not yet↑ a replacement for the diffusion or compute-reporting rule that reaches API customers as of 2026-10-22
    • Federal Register: BIS proposed rule, reporting requirements for advanced AI models and computing clusters (Sep 11, 2024) [82]

  • Not yet↑ know-your-customer duties for compute providers as of 2026-10-22
    • Egan & Heim: Oversight for Frontier AI through a KYC Scheme for Compute Providers (arXiv, Oct 2023) [79]
    • Federal Register: Commerce proposed IaaS KYC rule (Jan 29, 2024) [84]
  • Not yet↑ a statute making pre-release access mandatory as of 2026-10-22
    • White House: EO 14409, Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security (Jun 2, 2026) [94]
  • Not yet↑ a federal code of practice tied to a safe harbor as of 2026-10-22
    • European Commission — The General-Purpose AI Code of Practice (published Jul 10, 2025; signatories) [172]
    • Ball — A Framework for the Private Governance of Frontier Artificial Intelligence (arXiv, Apr 15, 2025) [200]
  • Not yet↓ further federal preemption of state rules without new duties as of 2026-10-22

H. A contestable national-champion program

12%range 5–25%±0 pts vs simulated 2026-09-30

A contestable national-champion program. The state funds several model builders and re-selects them in rounds on stated criteria, the way DOE re-competes site operators.[148][180] Korea's program dropped two of five teams in its first round, and a newly selected team receives compute, datasets and a "K-AI" designation.[180]

Resolves YES if

Resolves YES if by 2030-12-31 a US federal program funds at least three frontier-model developers with public compute or money and publishes re-selection or elimination rounds.

Signposts

  • Not yet↑ a US program that funds several frontier developers with public compute and published elimination criteria as of 2026-10-22
    • Korea Herald — LG, SKT, Upstage advance in Korea's sovereign AI project; Naver, NC dropped (Jan 15, 2026) [180]
  • Not yet↑ Genesis or CAISI awards tied to performance reviews rather than one-off grants as of 2026-10-22
    • DOE — First Genesis Mission projects selected (278 awards, Jul 22, 2026) [147]
  • Not yet↑ a federal definition of an 'independent' or 'domestic' model as of 2026-10-22
    • Korea Herald — LG, SKT, Upstage advance in Korea's sovereign AI project; Naver, NC dropped (Jan 15, 2026) [180]
  • Not yet↓ a single-champion bargain (path D) instead as of 2026-10-22
    • CNBC: Trump administration, OpenAI discussing possible government stake (Jun 5, 2026) [109]

Method and re-scoring rules (from the latest snapshot)
independence
Each probability is a separate estimate that its path resolves YES by the horizon. Paths can co-occur, so probabilities do not sum to 1; when one path's resolution makes another more or less likely, say so in the rationale rather than normalizing.
signpost mapping
Each signpost has a direction. not_yet->emerging moves the path roughly 0.03-0.05 in that direction; emerging->happened or not_yet->happened roughly 0.05-0.15, depending on how close the signpost is to the resolution criterion. A reversal (happened back to not_yet) moves it by the same amount the other way. Keep low<=point<=high and widen the range when evidence conflicts.
resolution
A path resolves YES when its resolution_criteria are met by a public document dated after 2026-10-22 and on or before the horizon; it resolves NO at the horizon otherwise. Record the resolving ledger id. A resolved path keeps probability 1 or 0 in later snapshots.
cadence
Re-score quarterly, and also within two weeks of any event that changes a signpost to 'happened'.

Why this horizon: Spans the next presidential term and a full contracting or export-licensing cycle, close enough that the 2026 record still bears on it, and matches stated delivery dates such as FugakuNEXT's 'around 2030' (ledger 177).
