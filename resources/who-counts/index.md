## My reading

The documents mostly say who writes the rules: 10 of the 12 rows answer that from a quotation. They say much less about who pays the checker (1 of 12) or what the checker loses if it is wrong (2 of 12). That is a finding about what these documents say, not about how the arrangements work in practice, and the pages may simply be the wrong place to look. It matters because the park-bill lesson turns on exactly those points: who wrote the definitions, who controlled the money, and when the authority ended. Where a company writes its own framework, the same company decides what counts as passing, and the table shows that without comment. I have not graded any row.

## Method

### How to read it

- Each row is one way AI is being checked. Each of the five questions is answered from the arrangement's own documents, with a quotation, a link and the date the page was read.
- "Not stated" means the pages read do not say. It is shown as plainly as any other answer, and it is not a verdict.
- The answers come from fixed lists written before any row was filled (listed below). The page does not grade, rank or total the rows, and the set of rows was chosen by hand, so counts across it are not a measurement of the field.

### Rules

- Rows are added by hand and kept once added. A row's id never changes.
- Every filled answer carries a verbatim quotation (checked by script against the saved page text), the page's address and the date it was read. Secondary sources are labeled as such and never carry a row alone.
- Where two sources disagree, both are shown in the row's note and the conflict is not resolved by guesswork.
- Snapshots are quarterly. Each is kept at its own address, and an earlier snapshot is never edited.
- Compiled with AI assistance. Every quotation is checked by script against the saved text of the page it came from; the judgment calls are the choice of rows and which listed answer a quotation supports.

### Disputing a row

To dispute a row, send a quotation from a primary document that contradicts it, through any of the profile links in the footer of the site's home page. A correction is added in the next snapshot with a dated note; earlier snapshots stay as they were.
