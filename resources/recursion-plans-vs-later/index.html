Recursion: what it said it would do, and what it says now

Recursion is one of the best-known AI-first drug discovery companies, and it has published more dated plans than most. This page sets a few of those plans, in its own words, beside what its later filings, calls and press coverage say.

How to read this. Every quote comes from a company filing, an earnings-call transcript, or press coverage quoting the company, and each is linked. Plans are compared with later statements, not with trial results, which this page does not judge. Filings are the company’s own text; interview transcripts are machine-made and not checked against audio. Research and commentary, not investment advice or a view on the stock.

A sentence that stops appearing

From the FY2021 through FY2024 annual reports, Recursion’s business section carries the same promise, word for word:

“Over time, and as our OS improves, we expect that moving failure earlier in the pipeline will result in an overall lower cost of drug development.”

It appears in the FY2021, FY2022, FY2023 and FY2024 reports. I did not find that sentence, or two shorter phrases from it, in the FY2025 report. A missing sentence is not a retraction, and I only searched for those phrases, so a reworded version could exist.

From “100 programs” to “promise to proof”

The CEO in 2024: “I think our pipeline will have 100 programs in it in the not-too-distant future.” He also gave a date to be judged by: “if we had 100 programs in our pipeline in 2027, let's say, and started reading out multiple Phase II programs that were efficacious, I think I would be really happy, even if a little bit late on that promise.” Separately, Fierce (May 2026) wrote that Gibson had built a reputation by promising 100 new medicines in 10 years, and that Gibson, in a blog post last month, called this a “maximalist bet” that was wrong “not in direction, but in timeline.” That is a different promise from the 100-programs figure above. The current CEO, speaking in December 2025: “Gone are the days where an interesting model is sufficient. We need models that improve the medicines that we're making.” In May 2026: “Ideas from biology are great, but if you can't make molecules, then where are my products?” On the Q2 2026 earnings call (transcript): “the real advantage isn't the model itself. It's our operating system. It's our operating model.”

My reading. The plan changed from more programs, sooner, toward fewer programs shown to work, and the claimed advantage moved from the size of the pipeline to the operating process around the models. The company says this itself; it is not an outside criticism. What the page cannot yet say is whether the new, smaller promises are being kept: for example, the Q2 2026 call says “we just received IND clearance for REC-7735, positioning it to enter the clinic later this year.” and that is checkable by the end of 2026.

My reading. Of the six items, three happened as planned, one happened in 2025 without a stated half-year, one was stopped, and one moved a year. That is my count of the company’s own list, not a measure of the science. Two of the on-plan items, the first patient dosed and an early data update, are the kinds of milestones a company controls more than it controls results; the stopped and moved items were decided in the May 2025 reprioritization. The “up to 10 additional clinical program milestones” figure in the same report is a total this table does not reproduce: the report names six in the list above, and I did not try to find the other four. The 18 months run to about August 2026, and the Q2 2026 call is the nearest later checkpoint.
