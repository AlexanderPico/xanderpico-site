ABOUT

About this site

I’m Alex Pico, an experimental scientist and software builder. This site is my independent research, writing and projects.

Earlier work: I co-founded WikiPathways, held a leadership role in Cytoscape and network biology, and directed bioinformatics at Gladstone Institutes. My papers are on the publications page and under my ORCID record.

How the site is organized

  • Threads are ongoing projects, each with one guiding question.
  • Resources are what a thread has produced so far: articles, results, tables, trackers and newsletters.
  • Records are the outside sources a thread draws on, each linked to where it was published.

Pages marked “in development” or “work in progress” can change. Each page shows when it was published and last updated. Trackers keep every dated snapshot at a permanent address.

How the work is made

I work with AI research assistants that I run locally through a customized harness that lets them build on earlier work without retraining. They gather sources, run experiments I design, and draft text and estimates. I choose the questions, review and revise what is published, and am responsible for it.

Where a page says “the research team”, “we”, or “draft estimates”, it means me working with those assistants. Estimates marked as drafts or “unreviewed machine readings” have not been checked line by line; the page says so.

Podcast voices are AI-generated, and each episode names the tools used.

Independence

I do not receive compensation from the companies discussed here. Nothing on this site is investment advice.

Using and citing this work

  • You may quote and link to anything here with attribution.
  • Suggested citation: Alex Pico. “Page title.” xanderpico.com, publication date. Page address. Each resource page carries its own ready-made citation line.
  • © Alex Pico, all rights reserved. Quoting, summarizing and linking with attribution are welcome; please ask before republishing whole pages.
  • AI assistants may read, summarize and cite these pages when someone asks them to.

For AI agents and feed readers

  • llms.txt: a short index of the site.
  • site.json: every thread with its findings, resources, records and related threads.
  • feed.xml and feed.json: everything new, newest first. Each thread also has its own feed.
  • Every thread and resource page has a Markdown copy, and tables are available as CSV.
